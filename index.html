Fleet command · The Solar War, 753 PCE

753 PCE. Ten years after the Rising, three powers fight for the Solar System. Choose one, and win the war week by week and battle by battle.

The war so far

753 PCE, the tenth year of the Solar War. The Solar Republic rules from Luna and holds Earth, Mars and Ceres; its legions are fighting for Mercury. The Society Remnant holds Venus and Mercury, where the Ash Lord lies ailing and Atalantia commands in his name. The Rim Dominion has kept ten years of peace with the Republic, and watches from Io. Each turn is a week. Build up your worlds, lay down hulls in your yards and move fleets between the planets. When fleets meet, take command and fight the battle in real time, or let it resolve. Break a world’s defenders, hold orbit for a week, then drop the Iron Rain to take it. Hold the capital of every power that is not your ally to win the war.

The Solar War, ten years on. One continuous map of the whole Solar System, forty-five worlds from Mercury out to Eris and the Kuiper dwarfs, with every orbit at its true relative distance. The Solar Republic holds Earth, Luna, Mars and Ceres and besieges Mercury; the Society Remnant holds Venus and Mercury; the Rim Dominion holds Jupiter, Saturn and the outer giants. Whichever side nobody plays keeps its worlds as armed neutrals. Every world pays out its own resources and carries its own benefits and downsides. Take a world by boarding its orbital authority. Ships far from their destination open a transit burn and cross between worlds in a minute or two. Lose your citadel and you lose the war.

Sensors and the approachFleets start 100,000 km apart. A ship with her shields up shows on every scope across the field, so you see the enemy from the start as contacts (an amber diamond: size and heading, rough position). To shoot you need a lock: corvettes 9,000 km, frigates and torchShips 12,000, destroyers 14,000, dreadnoughts 20,000, the moonBreaker 24,000, pickets 18,000. Any lock is shared, and every gun of the fleet can then fire to its full reach. Go cold (U): shields drain, drives drop to a whisper, guns hold, and she vanishes beyond a few thousand km; warm up (U again) and the guns are free at once, but the shields need 6 seconds (3 for the Rim) to spin up from empty. Near the Sun, anything sunward is half lost in the glare. A nuclear blast blinds sensors within 8,000 km for 15 seconds and hides everything inside 3,000 km. The approach runs at 12×; Skip to contact runs it faster still. The battle clock starts at contact. Pickets (in deployment) hear further and catch cold ships. The Solar System. Orbits around the Sun are to one true scale (1 AU = 4,000,000 km of map), so the gaps between worlds are right relative to each other. Each planet with its moons is magnified so it can be fought over; inside a planet system, sizes and moon distances are true to each other (Phobos and Deimos are drawn larger than life). Zoom out with the wheel to see the whole system; the minimap frames the inner system near the Sun and the whole system beyond the Belt. Worlds of the side nobody plays are neutral garrisons: they fire only on those who attack them, and can be boarded. Click a world to see what holding it gives you. A ship ordered more than 15,000 km away goes into a transit burn: its top speed and thrust scale with the length of the trip: about a minute and a half from Earth to Mars, three to Jupiter, four and a half to Neptune. It burns half again the fuel and sheds the speed before it arrives. Worlds pay He-3, ore, ordnance and crews every second to whoever holds their orbital authority (a citadel, Dockyard or Mining Station). These can't be destroyed, only boarded. Food is a balance: if your worlds can't feed themselves, recruitment falls 40%.
Four resources. He-3 pays for energy, fuel and part of every hull. Ore is hull metal: haulers mine it from rust-veined fields (most are at the edges of the asteroid fields), salvaged hulks give mostly ore, and depot hull repairs spend it. Ordnance is the magazine stockpile: the citadel, Dockyards and Mining Stations produce it, every rearm spends it, and a restocked nuke costs 200. Manpower is your pool of crews: building a ship takes its whole crew from the pool (a Dreadnought needs about 20,000), re-crewing at a depot draws more, and losses are permanent except for the 12% who reach the lifeboats. The citadel recruits steadily, owned Dockyards and Mining Stations add more, and taking a station frees its workers to join you. The Remnant recruits fastest, the Republic doubles what stations give, and Rim crews are 15% smaller.
Battle groups. A group flies at its slowest ship's pace and holds formation on its flagship, the most valuable ship aboard (a commander's ship wins ties). Order the whole group and it moves as one; give a few members their own orders and they detach until you order the whole group again. Formations: Wedge, Line abreast, Column, Wall, and Screen, which rings the capitals with escorts. Stances: Aggressive breaks formation to chase anything a little past the reach of its guns. Defensive holds formation and engages only what closes on the flagship. Hold fire keeps the main guns silent so the group runs quiet. Evasive spreads out and fights from 40% farther out. Plain selections use the formation you pick in the ship panel.
He-3 fuel. Every burn draws on the tank: a full tank gives about two and a half minutes of hard burn for most warships. Refuel at the citadel, a Dockyard or a Mining Station (it costs He-3), or from a Supply Tender. A dry ship drifts on momentum, and gravity takes it from there.
Loyalty. Hull damage, a destroyed bridge and a lost commander all erode crew loyalty. Gold-heavy Society crews resist; Rim crews break easiest. Once loyalty is under 20% and the hull under half, a ship near enemies may strike its colors. A warship that stays alongside for four seconds, or any boarder, takes her as a prize. Against the Republic, a breaking crew may simply mutiny and change sides. Republic boarders also call on the lowColors to rise while they fight.
Commanders. Destroyers and larger can carry one named commander, assigned at a friendly depot. Traits boost the helm, gunnery, discipline or boarding, and renown earns promotions. Commanders can die with their ship (40% reach an escape pod) or be captured with it, which pays the captor a 250 He-3 ransom and shakes the loser's fleet.
Armour and broadsides. Every hull carries armour plate: each slug or round loses that much on the plate (never below 15% of it), so light guns barely scratch a dreadnought, while lances, missiles, nukes and boarders ignore it. Shields come in two halves, port and starboard; a shot lands on the half it strikes, and with that half down it goes straight through to the hull.
Command. Orders reach a ship once the flagship's signal crosses to her: a fifth of a second to a second and a half, longer with a damaged bridge, at a distance from the flagship, under EMP or in a sky that jams. The Society's Gold command is quickest, but its captains hold station a few seconds when a flagship dies; the Republic's orders are slower, and nobody waits on a dead flagship; the Rim's hulls fade from sensors on a quiet burn.
Subsystems. Frigates and larger have engines, bridge, port and starboard gun decks and a shield generator. Hits land by facing: stern shots hit the engines, bow shots the bridge, a broadside the guns on that side. Damage that is not repaired in the battle stays with the ship until a yard puts it right. Fire control chips in the ship panel tell your gunners what to aim for. Knock out the shield generator and the ship cannot raise shields until repaired.
Veterancy. Ships earn renown by dealing damage and making kills: Blooded, Veteran, Scarred, each firing faster, tighter and steadier. Every successful boarding action adds to your legion's renown, which makes all your boarders fight harder.
Neutral ground. Derelict hulks drift in the wreckage of the long siege off Mercury: strip them with haulers or board them. Asteroid fields block fire and sensors, and ships inside them are hard to see.
